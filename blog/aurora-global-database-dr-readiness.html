DR Readiness for Aurora Global Database: From Architecture to Quarterly Drills

By Dishant Modh·Nov 2025·7 min read

How to make Aurora Global Database disaster recovery real: RPO and RTO targets, switchover vs failover, replication lag monitoring, runbooks and automated drills.

On this page

    A multi-region architecture is not a disaster recovery plan until you have failed over and measured how long it took. On a multi-region Aurora Global Database setup, combined with AWS DMS for a few specific flows, we pushed RPO from about 15 minutes to near zero and RTO from about 8 minutes to under 60 seconds, then proved it every quarter with automated drills. Here is the framework.

    1. Start with RPO and RTO as numbers

    RPO is how much data you can afford to lose; RTO is how long you can be down. Write them per service tier. A target nobody wrote down cannot be tested, and a target you never tested is a guess.

    2. What Aurora Global Database gives you

    Aurora Global Database replicates at the storage layer to read-only secondary clusters in other AWS Regions, typically with replication lag well under a second. Because replication does not compete with query processing on the primary, it is a good fit for cross-region DR and low-latency regional reads. Check the current documentation for the number of supported secondary Regions and engine-version requirements before you design.

    3. Switchover vs failover

    • Managed planned failover (switchover): a controlled role swap with no data loss, ideal for drills, maintenance and region rebalancing.
    • Unplanned failover: when the primary Region is impaired, you detach and promote a secondary cluster. Some in-flight data may not have replicated, which is exactly what your RPO target describes.

    Practise both. Planned switchovers are low risk and build muscle memory; unplanned ones test your real runbook.

    4. Monitor the things that predict your RPO

    • Cross-region replication lag (CloudWatch AuroraGlobalDBReplicationLag) with alarms well below your RPO.
    • For Aurora PostgreSQL, consider the rds.global_db_rpo parameter to bound RPO. Verify behaviour in current AWS docs, since it can affect write availability when lag exceeds the limit.
    • Replication health of any DMS tasks that feed or depend on the global cluster.

    5. The database is the easy part. Prepare the application

    • How do clients find the new writer? Use cluster endpoints with short DNS TTLs and connection retry logic.
    • Secrets, parameter groups, security groups and IAM must exist and match in the secondary Region. Manage them with Terraform so the secondary is not a hand-built snowflake.
    • Capacity: the secondary must handle full production load after promotion, so right-size or scale it in the runbook.
    • Dependencies: caches, queues and downstream services also need a regional story.

    6. Automate the drill

    Manual drills get skipped. Automating them with Lambda, CloudWatch and scripted runbooks turns DR from an annual fire drill into a routine check. A useful automated drill:

    1. Verifies replication lag is within target and the secondary is healthy.
    2. Executes a planned switchover (or promotes an isolated clone in a test account).
    3. Runs application health and write/read smoke tests against the new primary.
    4. Records the elapsed time as your measured RTO and checks data continuity as your measured RPO.
    5. Switches back and files a report. Failures open a ticket automatically.

    7. Mind the cost

    A warm secondary region means paying for compute, storage and cross-region data transfer. Review the instance sizes in the secondary regularly and align the DR tier with business value rather than defaulting everything to the most expensive option.

    DR readiness checklist

    1. RPO and RTO written per tier.
    2. Replication-lag alarms below RPO.
    3. Secondary infrastructure managed as code.
    4. Runbooks for planned and unplanned failover.
    5. Automated drills on a schedule, with measured results.
    6. Post-drill review and action items.
